I studied Electronics and Communication Engineering.

I'm now a React developer.

Here's what surprised me about the transition.

---

When I started learning frontend, I expected to start from scratch.

What I found instead: most of the mental models I already had.

**State machines.** In ECE, we model systems that transition between defined states. React components are state machines. `idle → loading → success → error` is the same kind of thinking as a circuit that moves between operational modes.

**Signal flow.** In electronics, you trace where a signal comes from and where it goes. In React, you trace where data comes from (props / state) and where it flows (down the component tree). One-directional data flow made immediate sense to me.

**Debugging by elimination.** Hardware debugging taught me to isolate variables. You don't change three things at once. You change one thing, measure, and then proceed. I debug React the same way — one hypothesis at a time.

---

What I had to unlearn: the pace.

In hardware, feedback loops are slow. You design, fabricate, test. It takes days.

In frontend, feedback is instant. You save the file, the browser updates. That immediacy was intoxicating.

It's why I chose this. Not because ECE was wrong for me — but because frontend was more right.

---

If you're an engineer from a non-CS background considering frontend:

Your fundamentals are not a disadvantage. They're just in a different language.

Translate them.
