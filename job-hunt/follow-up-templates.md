# Follow-Up Templates

> **The rule:** Follow up exactly once per stage, with added value each time.
> Following up shows initiative. Following up twice shows desperation. Know the difference.
>
> **Timing discipline is everything.** Send too early = looks desperate. Send too late = looks uninterested.

---

## Template 1 — After Applying (1 Week Later)

**When:** Exactly 7 days after submitting the application. No sooner.
**Goal:** Get visibility past the ATS and into a human's inbox.
**Where to send:** Find the hiring manager or recruiter on LinkedIn. Send there, not through the portal.
**Subject:** `Following up — Frontend Developer application — Dinesh S`

---

```
Subject: Following up — Frontend Developer application — Dinesh S

Hi [Name],

I applied for the Frontend Developer role at [Company] about a week ago and
wanted to follow up directly — I know applications can get buried.

I'm particularly interested in [Company] because [one specific reason —
their product, a blog post, something they recently shipped]. On the
technical side, I work primarily in React and TypeScript, and I've built
[one relevant project in one sentence — e.g., "a full analytics dashboard
with TanStack Query and Recharts that you can see at [link]"].

If my background looks like a fit, I'd love a 20-minute call. If the
timing is off or the role is filled, no worries at all.

Portfolio: [your-portfolio.vercel.app]
GitHub: github.com/DINESHTM07

Thanks for your time,
Dinesh S
duke02101@gmail.com
```

---

**What makes this work:**
- You didn't just say "I applied, please notice me" — you gave them something to click (portfolio)
- The specific reason you're interested proves you're not spray-applying
- You gave them an easy out ("no worries at all") — paradoxically makes them more likely to respond

**If no response after this:** Move it to "Ghosted" in your tracker. One follow-up is the limit.

## Template 2 — After Interview (Same Day)

**When:** Within 2 hours of any interview — phone screen, technical round, or final round.
**Goal:** Stay top of mind, demonstrate genuine interest, close any gaps.

product direction, a challenge they mentioned.]

I'm genuinely excited about the opportunity. Looking forward to the next
steps.

Best,
Dinesh S
duke02101@gmail.com | [your-portfolio.vercel.app]
```

I'm excited about the role and looking forward to hearing about next steps.

Dinesh S
duke02101@gmail.com | [your-portfolio.vercel.app]

### 2C — After Final Round

```
Subject: Thank you — [Your Name] / Final interview — [Company]

Hi [Name],

Thank you for today — I really appreciated the conversation, especially
[something specific: a question they asked, something about the team
culture, a product discussion].

After speaking with [name of interviewers if multiple rounds], I feel

next chapter of my career. [One sentence on why — specific, not generic.]

**Reminder:** If multiple people interviewed you, send individual emails to each person. Personalize every one. A group "thanks all" email reads as lazy.

## Template 3 — After Rejection (Asking for Feedback)

**When:** You receive a rejection at any stage (phone screen, technical, final).
**Goal:** Get actionable feedback to improve. Secondary goal: leave a positive impression (they may think of you for future roles).
**Timing:** Send within 24 hours of receiving the rejection.
**Tone:** Zero bitterness. Genuine gratitude. Specific ask.

---

### 3A — After Early-Stage Rejection (Resume / Phone Screen)

```
Subject: Re: [Your Application / Interview] — Feedback Request

Hi [Name],

Thank you for letting me know — I appreciate the response.

I'd love to use this as a learning opportunity. If you have 2 minutes,
would you be willing to share any feedback on where I could improve my
application or profile? Even one sentence would be genuinely helpful.

I'll absolutely respect whatever you can and can't share.

Thanks again,
Dinesh S
```

---

### 3B — After Technical Round Rejection

```
Subject: Re: Frontend Developer — Thank you + feedback request

Hi [Name],

Thank you for the update — I appreciate you closing the loop.

I found the technical round genuinely challenging and educational. If
it's possible, I'd love any feedback on where I fell short technically —
even a general area (e.g., state management, performance, system design)
would help me focus my learning.

I'll completely understand if that's not something you can share.

Either way — thank you for the time and the experience. I'd love to be
considered again if a relevant role opens up.

Best,
Dinesh S
[your-portfolio.vercel.app]
```

---

### 3C — After Final Round Rejection

```
Subject: Re: Frontend Developer — Thank you

Hi [Name],

Thank you for letting me know — and for the genuine time and
conversation throughout the process.

I'll be honest: I was disappointed, but I have a lot of respect for how
[Company] ran the process. It was one of the better interview experiences
I've had.

If you're open to it, I'd really value any feedback on what ultimately
made the difference — not to dispute the decision, but to understand
where to grow. Even a sentence or two would mean a lot.

I'd love to stay in touch, and I hope our paths cross again.

Dinesh S
duke02101@gmail.com | [your-portfolio.vercel.app]
```

---

**What to do with feedback:**
- Write it down in your Rejection Log (`application-tracker.md`)
- Look for patterns after 5+ rejections — that's where your real gap is
- Don't argue with feedback. Thank them and improve.

**Reality check:** Most companies won't give detailed feedback for legal reasons. That's fine. The ask is still worth it — it leaves a positive impression, and some will surprise you with useful detail.

**Tone:** Enthusiastic, clear, professional. No excessive gushing.

## Template 4B — Declining an Offer

**When:** You've decided to go elsewhere or the terms don't work.
**Tone:** Gracious, brief, no over-explanation. The tech world is small.
**Rule:** Never burn a bridge. The CTO of the company you're declining may be at your dream company in 2 years.

---

```
Subject: Re: Offer — Dinesh S / Frontend Developer

Hi [Name],

Thank you so much for the offer and for the time your team invested in the
process. It was genuinely a difficult decision.

After careful consideration, I've decided to move forward with another
opportunity that's a closer fit for where I want to grow right now.

I have real respect for [Company] and the team I met. I hope our paths
cross again in the future — and I'll be rooting for what you're building.

With gratitude,
Dinesh S
```

---

**What NOT to say when declining:**
- Don't mention the other company by name
- Don't give detailed reasons ("the salary was too low") — it invites counter-offers you may not want
- Don't apologize excessively — one thank-you is enough
- Don't say "I'll keep you in mind for the future" unless you mean it

## Timing Reference

| Action | When to Send |
|---|---|
| After applying | Exactly 7 days later |
| After phone screen | Same day, within 2 hours |
| After technical round | Same day, within 2 hours |
| After final round | Same day, within 1 hour |
| After rejection | Within 24 hours |
| Accepting an offer | Within the deadline they gave |
| Declining an offer | As soon as you're decided — don't make them wait |

---

## The 48-Hour Rule

If you haven't heard back within the stated timeline:

> After they say "you'll hear from us in X days" — wait X+2 days before following up.

If they said "we'll decide by Friday" and it's now Tuesday — send a brief check-in:

```
Hi [Name],

I wanted to check in — I understood a decision might be ready around
[date]. Happy to wait if the timeline has shifted, just wanted to make
sure nothing was needed from my side.

Dinesh
```
