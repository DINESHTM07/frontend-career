# Cold Email & LinkedIn Message Templates

> **How to use:** Every template has `[BRACKETS]` for things you must personalize.
> The parts outside brackets should be kept mostly as-is — they're crafted for tone.
> Personalization = 1 specific detail about their company/product. Generic = deleted.

---

## Template 1 — Cold Email to Hiring Manager at a Startup

**When:** You found a frontend role posted on LinkedIn, AngelList, or their careers page.
**Tone:** Direct, confident, zero fluff. Startup hiring managers read fast.
**Subject line options:**
- `Frontend dev with React + TypeScript — [Your Name]`
- `Re: Frontend Developer role — quick intro`
- `React dev who actually finishes things — [Your Name]`

---

```
Subject: Frontend dev with React + TypeScript — Dinesh S

Hi [First Name],

I came across [Company]'s frontend role and wanted to reach out directly
rather than disappear into an ATS.

Quick context: I'm a self-taught React developer with a background in ECE.
Before I wrote code, I wrote a 250,000-word fantasy novel and self-published
it — which is my honest proof that I start ambitious things and finish them.
On the technical side, I've built an analytics dashboard, an e-commerce
store, and a component-rich portfolio using React, TypeScript, Tailwind, and
Framer Motion.

One thing I noticed about [Company]: [specific observation — e.g., "your
onboarding flow is React-based but the dashboard seems to lag on mobile —
that's exactly the kind of performance problem I enjoy solving"].

I'd love a 20-minute call if you think there's a fit.

Portfolio: [your-portfolio.vercel.app]
GitHub: github.com/DINESHTM07

Best,
Dinesh S
duke02101@gmail.com
```

---

**Personalization checklist before sending:**
- [ ] Used their first name (find it on LinkedIn if not in the job post)
- [ ] Named something specific about their product, stack, or company
- [ ] Portfolio link is live and working
- [ ] Subject line is clean — no "Regarding" or "Application for"

## Template 2 — Cold LinkedIn Message to CTO

**When:** Small startup (under 50 people), no active job post, or you want to reach someone above the recruiter.
**Tone:** Even shorter. CTOs get 50+ messages a week. 4 sentences maximum.
**Character limit:** LinkedIn connection requests = 300 chars. InMail = longer.

---

**Connection request note (300 chars max):**

```
Hi [Name] — I'm a React/TypeScript developer who studied ECE and
self-published a 250K-word novel (my proof I finish hard things).
I admire what [Company] is building with [specific product/feature].
Would love to connect — no ask yet, just respect for the work.
```

---

**Follow-up message after they accept (send within 24 hours):**

```
Thanks for connecting, [Name].

I'll be direct: I'm looking for a frontend role and [Company] is near the
top of my list. I've been following [specific thing — their product launch,
a blog post they wrote, a feature you saw them ship].

I build in React + TypeScript. Here's my portfolio if you're curious:
[your-portfolio.vercel.app]

If there's ever a fit, I'd love 15 minutes. No pressure if not — happy to
stay connected either way.
```

---

**Why this works:** You made the first message about them, not you. The second message leads with directness (they respect it) and ends without pressure.

## Template 3 — Follow-Up After No Response (5 Days Later)

**When:** You sent the initial cold email or applied through their portal 5–7 days ago. Zero response.
**Tone:** Light, zero guilt-tripping, adds new value.
**Subject:** Reply to your original thread (keeps it in the same conversation).

---

```
Subject: Re: Frontend dev with React + TypeScript — Dinesh S

Hi [First Name],

Following up in case this got buried.

Since my last email I shipped [one thing — e.g., "added real-time chart
filtering to my dashboard project — here's a 60-second Loom if helpful:
[loom link]"].

Still interested in [Company] — happy to jump on a quick call whenever
works for you.

Dinesh
```

---

**Rules for follow-ups:**
- One follow-up only. Two maximum over 3 weeks. After that — move on.
- Always add something new (a project update, a link, a relevant observation).
- Never: "I just wanted to circle back" or "I know you're busy but…"
- Never guilt-trip or mention how long you've waited.

## Template 4 — Thank You Email After Interview

**When:** Within 2 hours of the interview ending. Same day at absolute latest.
**Tone:** Warm, specific, brief. Not a form letter — they'll know if it is.
**Subject:** `Thank you — [Your Name] / [Role] interview`

---

```
Subject: Thank you — Dinesh S / Frontend Developer interview

Hi [Interviewer's First Name],

Thank you for the time today — I genuinely enjoyed the conversation,
especially [one specific thing you discussed: a technical problem, a product
decision they explained, something they said about the team].

It reinforced that [Company] is exactly the kind of place I want to build my
career. [One sentence on why — specific, not generic.]

I'm attaching my portfolio link again for reference: [your-portfolio.vercel.app]

Looking forward to hearing about next steps. Feel free to reach out if you
have any follow-up questions.

Best,
Dinesh S
duke02101@gmail.com | [your-portfolio.vercel.app]
```

---

**If you had a technical interview and got a question wrong:**

Add this paragraph before the closing:

```
One thing I want to revisit: when you asked about [topic], I said [what you
said]. After reflecting, the more accurate answer is [correct answer]. Wanted
to close that loop.
```

This shows intellectual honesty and that you keep thinking after the room ends. Interviewers notice.

---

**If multiple people interviewed you:** Send individual emails to each person. Personalize each one with something specific they said. Do not send a group email.

## Template 5 — Referral Request to Someone at Target Company

**When:** You know someone (even loosely) who works at a company you want to join.
**Tone:** Warm, specific, zero entitlement. Make it easy to say yes.
**Rule:** Never ask for a referral in the first message. Build context first.

---

**Step 1 — First message (no ask):**

```
Hi [Name],

I hope you're well. I've been following your work at [Company] and I've
been really impressed by [something specific — a feature, a blog post,
their engineering culture content on LinkedIn].

I'm currently in a career transition — I've been self-teaching React and
TypeScript for the past 8 months and I'm actively looking for my first
frontend role. Would love to grab a 15-minute virtual coffee to hear about
your experience at [Company] — just the culture, the work, what you find
challenging. No agenda beyond learning.

If that's too much to ask, completely understood.

Thanks,
Dinesh
```

---

**Step 2 — Referral ask (only after they agree to talk or respond warmly):**

```
Hi [Name],

Thanks again for [the call / the advice / your time last week].

I wanted to ask — and please say no if it feels off — [Company] has a
[Role] opening that looks like a strong fit for me. Would you be comfortable
referring me internally? I know referrals carry your name, so I'd only ask
if you felt confident after our conversation.

I've put together everything you'd need to share:
- Portfolio: [your-portfolio.vercel.app]
- GitHub: github.com/DINESHTM07
- Resume: [link to PDF]
- The specific role: [link to job posting]

Whatever you decide, I really appreciate the time you've given me.

Best,
Dinesh
```

---

**Why the two-step approach works:**
- You built a real interaction before asking for a favor
- You made the referral easy: everything they need is in one message
- You gave them an out ("please say no if it feels off") — paradoxically makes them more likely to say yes
- You showed you understand their reputation is on the line

## Quick Reference — Which Template for Which Situation

| Situation | Template |
|---|---|
| Job posted, have hiring manager name | Template 1 — Cold Email |

| Sent email 5+ days ago, no reply | Template 3 — Follow-Up |
| Just finished any interview | Template 4 — Thank You (send same day) |
| Know someone at target company | Template 5 — Referral (two-step) |
