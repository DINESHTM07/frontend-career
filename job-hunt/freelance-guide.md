# Freelance Guide — Finding Clients, Pitching, Pricing, Contracts

> **Why freelance while job hunting?**
> Real clients = real projects = real portfolio = stronger job applications.
> A ₹5,000 website you built for a local restaurant is more credible than your 10th tutorial clone.
> You don't need to be senior. You just need to be better than nothing — and most local businesses have nothing.

---

## Part 1 — Finding Local Businesses That Need Websites

### Where to Look (Thanjavur / Tamil Nadu)

**Walk the street, literally:**
- Hotels, lodges, homestays — most have zero web presence
- Restaurants and mess — especially ones with good food but no Swiggy/Zomato
- Coaching centres and private tutors
- Clinics, doctors, dentists — especially younger practitioners
- CA firms, advocates, consultants
- Tailors, boutiques, handloom shops
- Wedding photographers who only post on Instagram
- Small retailers who sell locally but want to expand online

**Online discovery:**
- Google Maps: search "restaurants Thanjavur" → filter by those with no website listed or only a Facebook page
- JustDial listings with no website link
- Instagram businesses with 500–5,000 followers but no website in their bio
- Facebook Business pages that look outdated
- Local WhatsApp groups and community boards

**Signs a business is ready:**
- They have a Facebook page but it's been dormant for 6+ months
- Their competitor has a website and they don't
- They're in a growth phase (new location, new service)
- They're asking "how do I get more customers?" — every business owner is

---

### Targeting Criteria (Focus Your Time)

Don't pitch everyone. Pick businesses where:

1. **They have something to show** — photos, a menu, a list of services
2. **They lose money without a website** — enquiries going to competitors, can't be found on Google
3. **The owner is reachable** — you can walk in or find their number on JustDial

Best first targets: **local restaurants, coaching centres, clinics, wedding photographers**. They're common, they understand the value of visibility, and they're used to paying for services.

## Part 2 — Pitch Templates

### In-Person Pitch (Walk-In)

> You walk into a restaurant. You've already checked — they have no website.

**Opening:**
> "Hi, I'm Dinesh. I'm a web developer based here in Thanjavur. I was looking you up online to recommend you to a friend, and I noticed you don't have a website. Do you have 2 minutes?"

**If they say yes:**
> "I'm building my portfolio right now and I'm looking for 2–3 local businesses to build websites for — completely free, no catch. All I need is a testimonial if you're happy with the work. I've built [mention 1–2 portfolio projects briefly]. Can I show you what I have in mind for you?"

**If they're hesitant:**
> "There's no commitment on your side. I'll build it, show it to you — if you like it, it's yours for free. If you don't, no problem, nothing changes for you."

## Part 3 — Pricing Guide (Future Paid Work)

Start free for 2–3 portfolio projects. After that, charge. Here's the framework:

### Pricing Tiers

| Tier | What's Included | Price Range | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Basic** | 3–5 pages, no CMS, static | ₹3,000–₹8,000 | Clinics, coaches, small shops |
| **Standard** | 5–8 pages, contact form, Google Maps, basic SEO | ₹8,000–₹18,000 | Restaurants, photographers |
| **Pro** | 8+ pages, CMS (so client can edit), blog, gallery | ₹18,000–₹35,000 | Businesses with regular updates |
| **E-commerce** | Product listings, cart, payment integration | ₹35,000–₹80,000 | Retailers, boutiques |
| **Monthly retainer** | Hosting, updates, minor changes | ₹1,000–₹3,000/month | Any existing client |

### How to Quote

1. Understand what they actually need (not what they say they want)
2. Add 30% buffer to your time estimate — clients always change things
3. Quote a project price, not an hourly rate — hourly creates scope anxiety for both sides
4. Split payment: 50% upfront, 50% on delivery
5. Hosting is not included in the site price — explain this separately

### What to Charge Extra For
- Domain purchase assistance: ₹500 service fee
- Hosting setup: ₹500–₹1,000 one-time
- Logo design: Either refer out or charge ₹2,000–₹5,000
- Content writing (they don't provide text): ₹500–₹1,500 per page
- Rush delivery (under 1 week): 25% premium

## Part 4 — Simple Contract Template

> **Why a contract, even for free work?** It defines what you're building, prevents scope creep, and protects both sides.
> For free projects, use a lightweight version. For paid work, use the full version.

---

### Lightweight Agreement (Free Portfolio Project)

```
PROJECT AGREEMENT
─────────────────────────────────────────

Client:           [Business Name]
Client Contact:   [Name, Phone, Email]
Developer:        Dinesh S — duke02101@gmail.com
Date:             [Date]
Project:          Website for [Business Name]

WHAT I WILL BUILD
─────────────────────────────────────────
[ ] Home page
[ ] [Services / Menu / About] page
[ ] Contact page with form and Google Maps
[ ] Mobile-responsive design
[ ] Basic on-page SEO (page titles, meta descriptions)

WHAT I WILL NOT BUILD (unless separately agreed)
─────────────────────────────────────────
✗ Payment gateway / e-commerce
✗ Login / user accounts
✗ Custom animations beyond basic effects
✗ Logo design
✗ Content writing (client provides all text and photos)

WHAT THE CLIENT PROVIDES
─────────────────────────────────────────
• Business name, address, phone, email
• All photos and images to be used
• Text/copy for all pages
• Logo (if they have one)
• Feedback within 3 business days of each version shared

TIMELINE
─────────────────────────────────────────
First version shared:    Within [2 weeks] of receiving all content
Revisions:               Up to 2 rounds included
Final delivery:          Within [3 weeks] of project start

TERMS
─────────────────────────────────────────
• This project is provided free of charge as a portfolio piece
• Developer retains the right to display this project in their portfolio
• Client agrees to provide a written testimonial if satisfied
• Hosting and domain are the client's responsibility after handoff
• Developer is not responsible for ongoing maintenance

Agreed by:

Client Signature: ________________   Date: ________

Developer:        Dinesh S           Date: ________
```

## Part 5 — How to Manage a Freelance Project

### The 5-Phase Process

**Phase 1 — Discovery (Day 1–2)**
- Meet or call the client
- Ask: Who are your customers? What do you want them to do on the site? Do you have a competitor's website you like?
- Get all content: text, photos, logo, contact details
- Agree on timeline and deliverables in writing (the agreement above)

**Phase 2 — Design (Day 3–5)**
- Build a basic wireframe (even on paper) — show page layout before you code
- Pick colors based on their existing brand or ask preferences
- Get one quick approval before coding starts

**Phase 3 — Build (Day 5–12)**
- Code the site
- Test on mobile (your phone + Chrome DevTools)
- Test the contact form
- Check all links and images

**Phase 4 — Review (Day 12–15)**
- Share a preview link (Vercel or Netlify preview URL)
- Give the client specific questions: "Does this color feel right?" "Is the text accurate?" Not just "what do you think?"
- Collect feedback in writing (WhatsApp is fine — screenshot it)
- Make up to 2 rounds of revisions

**Phase 5 — Handoff (Day 15–18)**
- Transfer domain/hosting to their account (or set up under theirs from the start)
- Record a 5-minute Loom video showing how to update basic content (if using a CMS)
- Get the testimonial in writing before you hand over credentials
- Add the project to your portfolio (`data/projects.js`)

---

### Managing Client Expectations

**The most common problems:**

| Problem | Prevention |
|---|---|
| "Can you add X?" (scope creep) | Written agreement listing what's included |
| Client disappears mid-project | Get all content before starting Phase 3 |
| "I don't like it" without specifics | Ask specific questions during review |
| Late feedback | Set a deadline: "I need feedback by [date] to stay on timeline" |
| "Can you update it for free?" (forever) | Offer a ₹1,000–₹2,000/month retainer for updates |

**The one rule:** Put everything in writing. WhatsApp messages count. Email counts. "We discussed" does not.
