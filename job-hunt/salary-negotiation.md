# Salary Negotiation — Scripts and Strategy

> **The core principle:** Everything before the offer is about getting the offer.
> Everything after the offer is about negotiating it. Don't mix the two.
>
> **The most important rule:** Never give a number first. Whoever speaks first loses.

---

## Before You Negotiate — Know Your Numbers

Fill this in before any interview process:

```
My walk-away number (minimum I'll accept):       ₹ ____________ / year
My target number (what I actually want):          ₹ ____________ / year
My stretch number (ask this, expect less):        ₹ ____________ / year

Market range for React dev (fresher/0-1 yr):
  - Startups (funded, Series A+):    ₹3.5L – ₹6L
  - Product companies (Tier 2):      ₹3L – ₹5L
  - Service companies (WITCH):       ₹2.8L – ₹4.5L
  - Remote (USD companies):          $15 – $35/hr (freelance)

Non-salary items I care about:
  [ ] Work from home days
  [ ] Learning budget / conference allowance
  [ ] Joining bonus
  [ ] Variable / performance bonus structure
  [ ] Early performance review (ask for 6-month review instead of 12)
```

## Script 1 — Deflecting the Salary Question (Before Offer)

**When:** HR asks "What are your salary expectations?" during the screening call.
**Goal:** Not give a number yet. Keep leverage.

---

**Option A — Flip it back:**

> "I'd love to understand the full scope of the role before throwing out a number — I want to make sure we're aligned on what I'd be doing day-to-day. Could you share the budgeted range for this position?"

*Most companies have a range. If they share it, you now know their ceiling.*

---

**Option B — Give a range (if they push hard):**

> "Based on my research into similar React roles in [city/remote], I'm targeting somewhere in the ₹[X]L–₹[Y]L range — but I'm more interested in the right fit than hitting a specific number. I'm flexible if the role and team are the right match."

*Set your floor as the bottom of the range, not the middle. They'll anchor to the bottom.*

---

**Option C — If they say "we need a number today":**

> "Understood. I'll say ₹[stretch number]L, but I want to be clear that's a starting point for conversation, not a take-it-or-leave-it. I'd rather talk about what the role is worth to you first."

---

## Script 2 — Counter-Offer After a Low Offer

**When:** They've made an offer that's below your target number.
**Rule:** Express enthusiasm first. Then counter. Never reject on the call.
**Timing:** Take at least a night before responding, even if you're ready.

---

**Initial response on the call (buy time):**

> "Thank you — I'm genuinely excited about this opportunity. Before I respond formally, I'd like a day to review everything carefully. Can I get back to you by [tomorrow / day after tomorrow]?"

---

**Counter-offer email:**

```
Subject: Re: Offer — [Your Name] / Frontend Developer

Hi [Name],

Thank you for the offer — I'm really excited about joining [Company] and
working on [specific thing you're excited about].

I've reviewed the package carefully. The role and team are exactly what I'm
looking for. On the compensation side, I was hoping we could get closer to
₹[target number]L. Here's my reasoning:

- The market rate for React + TypeScript frontend developers in [city/remote]
  sits between ₹[X]L and ₹[Y]L based on my research
- My portfolio demonstrates production-quality work across multiple projects
  (portfolio: [link]) and I'd be contributing from week one
- [One more specific point — e.g., "I've already studied your tech stack
  and can skip the ramp-up most juniors need"]

I understand there may be constraints, and I'm not expecting you to meet me
exactly. If ₹[target]L isn't possible, I'm open to discussing other
structures — a 6-month performance review with a salary adjustment trigger,
or a joining bonus, for example.

I'm genuinely excited about this role and I'd love to make it work.

Best,
Dinesh
```

---

**If they say the number is fixed:**

> "I appreciate you being direct. Can we talk about the other parts of the package — specifically, is there flexibility on the joining date, remote work days, or an early performance review at 6 months?"

*Always find something to negotiate. Even if money is fixed, other things rarely are.*

## Script 3 — Negotiating a Joining Bonus

**When:** Base salary is fixed but you need money upfront (relocation, equipment, etc.) or want to bridge a gap.
**Joining bonuses are easier to give than salary** — they're one-time costs, not recurring.

---

**On the call:**

> "I appreciate the offer. The base salary is slightly below what I was targeting — before I respond on that, I wanted to ask: is there flexibility on a joining bonus? Even a one-time amount of ₹[X]K would help me bridge the gap and accept without hesitation."

---

**By email:**

```
Hi [Name],

Thank you again for the offer. I'm very interested in moving forward.

One thing I'd like to discuss: the base salary is a bit below my target.
I understand that base salary structures may have constraints. Would it be
possible to include a joining bonus of ₹[amount]? It would help me say yes
without reservation and start with full focus on the role rather than
financial logistics.

Happy to discuss — I want to find a structure that works for both of us.

Dinesh
```

---

**What's reasonable to ask:**
- Fresher / 0–1 yr experience: ₹20K–₹50K joining bonus is reasonable to ask
- If relocation is involved: ₹30K–₹75K relocation assistance is standard to request
- Never ask for more than 1 month's base salary as a joining bonus

---

## Script 4 — Asking for Time to Decide

**When:** You've received an offer but you're waiting on another process, or you genuinely need time.
**Rule:** You always have 24–72 hours minimum. Legitimate companies don't give exploding offers.

---

**On the call (immediate response):**

> "I'm really excited about this — thank you. I'd like to take [24/48] hours to review the full package carefully before giving you my answer. Can I get back to you by [specific day and time]?"

*Always give a specific time. "I'll get back to you soon" creates anxiety on both sides.*

---

**If you need more time (waiting on another offer):**

```
Hi [Name],

Thank you again for the offer — I'm genuinely excited about [Company] and
the role.

I want to be transparent: I'm in the final stages with one other company,
and out of respect for that process, I want to give them a chance to
conclude before I commit. I expect to have clarity by [date — be honest].

Would it be possible to have until [that date] to give you my decision?
I want to make a thoughtful choice, and [Company] is very much on my
shortlist.

Best,
Dinesh
```

---

**If they pressure you for an answer immediately:**

> "I understand the urgency and I want to be respectful of your timeline. I can commit by [earliest honest date]. I'm not trying to play games — I genuinely need [X days] to make a responsible decision. If that's not possible, I'd ask you to make that clear so I can adjust."

*If they withdraw the offer because you asked for 48 hours, you didn't want that job.*

## The BATNA Principle (Best Alternative to Negotiated Agreement)

Your negotiating power is entirely determined by your alternatives.

- **No other offer:** Your leverage is your skills + market research. Use market data as your argument.
- **One other offer:** Mention it vaguely ("I'm in conversations with other companies"). Don't use it as a threat.
- **One competing offer in hand:** You can say directly: "I have an offer for ₹[X]L. I'd prefer [Company] — can you match it?"

The best time to negotiate is when you have options. So apply widely. Every parallel process you run is leverage.

---

## After Negotiation — Getting It in Writing

Once you've agreed verbally:

> "Great — I'm really looking forward to this. Could you send over the revised offer letter with the updated [salary / joining bonus / work-from-home policy] so I can review and sign?"

**Never resign from or decline other offers until you have a signed offer letter.**
Verbal offers fall through. Written offers are real.

## Quick Reference

| Situation | What to say |
|---|---|
| They ask salary before offer | Flip it: "What's the budgeted range?" |
| Offer is low | Express excitement → ask for a night → counter by email |
| Base is fixed | Negotiate joining bonus, remote days, early review |
| Need more time | Give a specific date. Be honest if you have another offer. |
| They say final offer | "Can we look at the non-salary elements?" |
| They push back hard | "I understand. Let me think about this and get back to you." |
