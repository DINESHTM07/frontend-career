## The STAR Framework (know this cold)

- **S**ituation: Set the scene. 1-2 sentences. Context only.
- **T**ask: What was YOUR responsibility? What were you trying to accomplish?
- **A**ction: What did YOU specifically do? (Not "we" — "I"). This is the longest part.
- **R**esult: What happened? Quantify if possible. What did you learn?

**Common mistake:** Spending 80% of the answer on Situation and Task, then rushing through the Action and Result. The interviewer cares most about what YOU did and what happened.

## Morning (8:00 – 11:00 AM) — Stories 1-5: Work Ethic + Learning + Challenge

### Practice method for each story:

1. Read the story from `interview-vault/04-behavioral-interview.md`
2. Close the file
3. Tell the story out loud — aim for 90-120 seconds
4. Score yourself:
   - Does it follow STAR? ✅/❌
   - Is it specific enough? ✅/❌
   - Did you say "I" not "we"? ✅/❌
   - Does the result sound real? ✅/❌
5. Open the file — compare to your written version
6. Tell it again, fixing what you scored ❌

### Common behavioral questions to practice with these stories:

- "Tell me about a time you had to learn something quickly."
- "Tell me about a time you faced a difficult technical challenge."
- "Tell me about a project you're most proud of."
- "Tell me about a time something didn't go as planned."
- "Describe a time you went beyond what was required."

**What makes answers weak:**
> Vague language ("I worked really hard", "it was challenging"), no specific result, saying "we" when the interviewer wants to know what YOU did, stories that don't end with a concrete outcome.

**What makes answers strong:**
> Specific actions ("I refactored the component to use React.memo, which dropped re-renders from 40 to 4 per interaction"), clear ownership ("I was responsible for..."), real results ("the Lighthouse score went from 67 to 94"), honest lessons ("I learned that...").

## Midday (11:20 AM – 1:30 PM) — Stories 6-10: Teamwork + Conflict + Failure

### Practice method: same as morning, but focus on these questions:

- "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate or manager."
- "Tell me about a time you failed at something."
- "Tell me about a time you had to work with someone difficult."
- "Tell me about a time you gave or received constructive feedback."
- "Describe a time you had to prioritize competing tasks."

**How to handle conflict questions:**
> Don't trash the other person. Focus on: what you did, how you communicated, what the outcome was. Interviewers want to see emotional maturity. Format: "I disagreed because X. I scheduled a conversation where I explained my reasoning. We ended up doing Y, which resulted in Z."

**How to handle failure questions:**
> Don't pick a non-failure disguised as a strength ("my biggest weakness is I work too hard"). Pick a real failure, show genuine ownership, then show what you learned and how you've changed. Interviewers respect honesty about failure far more than a polished non-answer.

## Afternoon (1:30 – 3:00 PM) — Record 3 Stories + Improve + DSA

### Record yourself

Pick the 3 stories you felt least confident about. Record each one on your phone or webcam.

**Watch the recording and score:**
- Eye contact / looking away too much? (if on video)
- Too many filler words ("um", "like", "so", "you know")?
- Does it come across as natural or rehearsed?
- Is the result clear and specific?
- Total time — is it under 2 minutes?

**After watching:** Tell the story again. Don't re-read the script. Just tell it better.

### Quick answers to have ready (asked in almost every interview):

**"Tell me about yourself."**
> This is your 90-second intro. Structure: where you came from → what you've built → what you're looking for. Example: "I'm a self-taught frontend developer. Over the past 12 months I've built 3 production-quality projects — an e-commerce app with React and Zustand, an analytics dashboard in TypeScript, and a Next.js app with server components. I've solved 100+ DSA problems and done mock interviews to prepare. I'm looking for a junior frontend role where I can contribute from day one and keep growing."

**"Why do you want this role?"**
> Be specific to the company. Research them before the interview. "I've been following [company] because [specific thing]. Your stack matches what I've been building with. I want to be in a place where I can..."

**"Where do you see yourself in 3 years?"**
> "I want to become a strong mid-level developer. I want to own features end-to-end, mentor others, and go deeper into [performance / accessibility / architecture — pick one you actually care about]."
